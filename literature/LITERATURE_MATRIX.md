# Literature Matrix: When Is Sequential RL Needed for MPLS Traffic Engineering?

Compiled 2026-10-06. This is the companion to `references.bib` (93 entries; the BibTeX keys used here match the keys in the bib).

**How entries were verified.** Each entry's title, authors, venue and year were checked against at least one authoritative source: the Crossref API (for anything with a DOI), the arXiv API, the RFC Editor's JSON metadata, or the publisher/proceedings page (USENIX, PMLR, NeurIPS proceedings, JMLR, OpenReview/ICLR, JAIR), which was reached through a web search. Statements in the "Main result" column come from the paper's abstract or from passages I read in the full text. Those read in full text were DOTE, Teal, CFR-RL and Learning to Route. Where a cell gives my own interpretation instead of the paper's claim, it is marked **(our reading)**. Cells marked "not checked" mean I did not verify that detail, so I left it out rather than guess.

**Project in one line.** This is a flow-level simulator of an 18-router MPLS backbone with 17 demands and k=4 Yen paths. Every 5 minutes the controller makes at most one masked move, out of Discrete(69) actions. Moves carry costs, a 3-step dwell time and protected-class masks, and traffic is exogenous. The comparison is MaskablePPO (gamma=0.995) against a masked neural contextual bandit (CB) with epsilon-greedy exploration, which amounts to gamma=0. Static SP, greedy and CSPF-style reoptimization serve as baselines. The headline finding is that the CB beats PPO in most scenarios.

## Novelty audit

### (a) Has anyone already shown that myopic, contextual-bandit or one-shot methods match or beat DRL for TE?

**Largely yes, for the standard TE formulation.** Be careful about how this is framed.

- **One-shot / myopic TE is already the SOTA design principle.** Teal (`xu2023teal`) states that TE allocations in one interval do not affect future intervals, and reduces RL to a one-step return. DOTE (`perry2023dote`) calls RL an "overkill" for WAN TE and reports that the RL-based scheme of `valadarsky2017learning` "has extremely poor TE quality even on Abilene", while one-shot stochastic optimization nearly matches an oracle. FIGRET and HARP (`liu2024figret`, `alqiam2024harp`) are also one-shot learned TE, without RL.
- **Much of the "DRL-TE" literature is a contextual bandit in disguise (our reading).** CFR-RL (`zhang2020cfrrl`) uses state = TM_t and maximizes E[r_t] with REINFORCE. Learning to Route conditions on a history of exogenous demand matrices that its actions do not affect. So "a CB beats DRL for TE" is partly a re-description of what these papers already do.
- **Adjacent networking domains already show that strong simple baselines erase DRL gains.** Examples are optical resource allocation (`doherty2025hype`, which re-implemented 5 landmark papers) and ABR in the real world (`yan2020puffer`). In general ML the same holds for random search (`mania2018random`), greedy CB (`bietti2021bakeoff`) and CB vs. Q-learning in hedging (`cannelli2023hedging`).
- **What I did not find:** a controlled, same-environment, equal-budget comparison of a masked neural CB (gamma=0) against PPO in a TE/MPLS environment. No paper I found studies whether TE with persistent routing state, move costs and hold-down timers needs sequential RL. Searches for "contextual bandit traffic engineering", "myopic/gamma=0 ablation routing RL", "RL for MPLS LSP placement/RSVP-TE" and "DRL unnecessary for TE" returned only the items above, patents, or generic DRL-routing papers without a myopic baseline. Absence of evidence is not proof. A targeted Google Scholar pass on "bandit" + "traffic engineering" (2023-2026) and on the citing papers of DOTE and Teal is still advisable before submission.

### (b) Has anyone measured the effective horizon or sequentiality of TE?

**Not that I found.** The diagnostic itself is established in ML: the effective horizon (`laidlaw2023effective`, `laidlaw2024stochastic`), "greedy on the random-policy Q is optimal" as a predictor of deep RL success, the myopic exploration gap (`dann2022epsgreedy`), and the horizon as complexity control (`jiang2015horizon`, `petrik2008biasing`, `amit2020discount`). The formal "short-window coupling" model for routing exists as congested bandits (`awasthi2022congested`). Teal and DOTE *assert* one-step structure from the problem definition (exogenous TMs, full re-optimization each interval); they do not *measure* it. No TE paper I found reports a gamma sweep, k-step lookahead gains, or an effective-horizon estimate.

### (c) What is plausibly new vs. what is replication

**Plausibly new (defensible if executed carefully):**
1. **A formulation in which TE is genuinely sequential.** Routing state persists, at most one LSP moves per step, moves cost something, and dwell/hold-down and protected-class masks apply. This makes the current path assignment an endogenous state variable, unlike Teal/DOTE/CFR-RL, where each interval is solved afresh. The contribution is asking whether this *operationally realistic* coupling (RSVP-TE make-before-break, stability timers as in `kandula2005texcp`) makes sequential RL necessary, and finding that mostly it does not.
2. **A measured sequentiality diagnostic for TE.** Examples include an effective-horizon or k-step-lookahead estimate computed from the simulator, a PPO gamma sweep including gamma=0, and the regret of the greedy-on-Q^random policy per scenario. These are correlated with when PPO beats the CB (e.g., failure and flash-crowd scenarios vs. diurnal ones). This applies Laidlaw et al.'s framework to a networking control problem, and I found no prior instance.
3. **Scenario-level characterization.** This means showing which traffic and failure regimes produce enough temporal coupling for PPO to win, if any do.

**Replication / confirmation, not novelty:**
- "Simple or myopic beats DRL for TE" in general (DOTE, Teal, Doherty et al., Puffer).
- "PPO is sensitive and can lose to simple methods" (Henderson, Engstrom, Andrychowicz, Mania).
- Using a regression-based CB with masking (Foster & Rakhlin; Huang & Ontañón).

**Skeptical caveats a reviewer will raise:**
- **Algorithm vs. horizon confound.** PPO losing may reflect PPO's optimization or sample inefficiency rather than a short horizon. This needs PPO with gamma=0 (same network, same budget) and a CB with k-step targets or n-step returns, so that horizon is varied while the algorithm is held fixed. An equal tuning budget is also required (`patterson2024empirical`).
- **Shaping leakage.** If the CB regresses the shaped reward r + gamma_s*Phi(s') - Phi(s), it is not myopic: it gets one-step lookahead on Phi for free. Ng et al.'s invariance does not hold at gamma=0. Report the CB on unshaped reward too, or show the shaping term is excluded.
- **Short-horizon by construction?** A 3-step dwell, the mask that blocks protected moves above 100% projected utilization, and the single-move limit may themselves suppress long-horizon effects. The finding may be about this action design rather than TE in general.
- **Lower gamma as regularizer.** A gamma=0 win is consistent with a sequential problem plus limited data (`jiang2015horizon`, `amit2020discount`). Claims should say "myopic suffices *empirically at this budget*", not "TE is not sequential".
- **External validity.** The study uses one 18-router topology and a flow-level model (`boltres2024packerl` shows flow-level conclusions may not transfer). Baselines should include an LP/min-MLU oracle or DOTE-style one-shot optimizer per interval, not only static SP, greedy and CSPF.
- **Candidate path construction matters** (`doherty2025hype`: path count and sort order change heuristic performance substantially). Report sensitivity to k and to Yen ordering.

## Gap statement

Learned traffic engineering has converged on two views. Systems papers such as Teal and DOTE treat each TE interval as an independent one-shot problem, because traffic is exogenous and routing is fully re-optimized each interval; they conclude that sequential RL is unnecessary or "overkill". A separate line of DRL-TE work (DRL-TE, CFR-RL, EfficientTE, RedTE) applies policy-gradient agents without testing whether a myopic learner would do as well. Several of these are effectively contextual bandits. Neither line considers the setting operators actually face with RSVP-TE/SR-TE: routing state persists, reconfiguration is incremental and costly, hold-down timers and SLA-protection constraints restrict moves, and the controller can change only a few LSPs per interval. In that setting the current LSP assignment is endogenous state, so the problem is a genuine MDP. Whether its temporal coupling is strong enough to justify long-horizon RL is an open empirical question. The ML tools to answer it exist (the effective horizon, discount-factor regularization, congested bandits), but nobody has applied them to TE. We fill this gap with a controlled, equal-budget comparison of masked PPO and a masked neural contextual bandit on an MPLS single-move formulation, plus a measured sequentiality diagnostic (gamma sweep, k-step lookahead and effective-horizon estimates) that predicts when sequential RL is worth its cost.
