\subsection{RQ4: How sequential is the decision problem?}
\label{sec:sequentiality}

Formally the problem is an MDP (\S\ref{sec:formulation}); the question is how
much the omitted future terms matter. On \SeqLiveStates{} states visited by
the greedy controller (diagnostic seeds 4001--4003, every fourth decision), we
roll the cloned simulator forward from every legal action for up to 24
intervals---the action, then no further change---and compare the action that
is best for the first interval with the action that is best over $H$
intervals (Fig.~\ref{fig:seqdiag}). This is the effective-horizon idea of
Laidlaw et al.~\citep{laidlaw2023effective} with a no-change base policy.

\emph{Under the true future}, the problem is far from myopic per decision: the
first-interval choice is the 24-interval choice in only
\SeqLiveAgreeHTwentyFour\,\% of states, it captures \SeqLiveCapturedHTwentyFour\,\%
of the available 24-interval gain, and in \SeqLiveSacrificeHTwentyFour\,\% of
states the 24-interval best move has a \emph{negative} immediate advantage.
\emph{Holding traffic and link state at their current values} during the same
rollouts, the picture reverses: agreement is \SeqFrozenAgreeHTwentyFour\,\%,
the myopic choice captures \SeqFrozenCapturedHTwentyFour\,\% of the gain, and
such ``sacrifice'' moves occur in \SeqFrozenSacrificeHTwentyFour\,\% of states.
The pattern holds in every scenario (Appendix~\ref{app:results}). Almost all
of the non-myopic value is therefore \emph{anticipation of exogenous change}
(traffic growth, bursts, failures and recoveries), not the trade-off between a
one-off move cost and a persistent gain. That value is visible to a learner
only to the extent that future traffic is predictable from the observation,
which contains current volumes but not the clock.

The same conclusion follows from using the rollouts as controllers. On the
first five test seeds (35 episodes), the clairvoyant one-interval oracle
reaches \LadderOraclehOne, against \LadderBandit{} for the bandit and
\LadderMilptrack{} for MILP-track; extending its lookahead to three or six
intervals adds only \OracleThreeMinusOne{} [\OracleThreeMinusOneLo,
\OracleThreeMinusOneHi] and \OracleSixMinusOne{} [\OracleSixMinusOneLo,
\OracleSixMinusOneHi]. The headroom above the learners lies in estimating the
\emph{immediate} consequence of a move more accurately (the oracle knows the
next five minutes of traffic exactly), not in planning further ahead.
