\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For an incremental, masked MPLS traffic-engineering problem with persistent
routing state, a masked contextual bandit beats MaskablePPO on every training
root and reaches the return of a per-interval MILP optimizer. Varying the
discount within each learner family points to the planning horizon rather than
the learning algorithm: PPO with $\gamma=0$ matches the bandit, PPO with
$\gamma=0.995$ settles into costly oscillations on most roots, and a
bootstrapped value learner does not improve on the bandit. Lookahead is worth
little here even with perfect foresight, because a controller that re-plans
every interval can react when traffic changes, and with traffic held fixed the
first interval's reward already ranks moves almost as a 24-interval criterion
does. Once a move's effect is delayed, a learner of the decision-time reward
fails. A sequential learner should therefore be justified by measured temporal
coupling---delayed effects, or exogenous dynamics that the controller can
predict---rather than assumed from the fact that the problem is formally an
MDP.
