\subsection{RQ1: Does sequential PPO outperform contextual learning?}
\label{sec:rq1}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports all policies on the 140 test episodes. The bandit
reaches a mean return of \TestBandit{} [\TestBanditLo, \TestBanditHi] over
\TestBanditRoots{} training roots and PPO \TestPPO{} [\TestPPOLo, \TestPPOHi]
over \TestPPORoots{}. On paired episodes the bandit's advantage is \Gap{}
(two-stage bootstrap [\GapLo, \GapHi]; $t$-interval over roots
[\GapTLo, \GapTHi]); it is positive on \GapRootsPositive{} of \GapRoots{} roots
and in \GapWinRate\,\% of paired episodes ($d_z=\GapDz$). The ordering does not
depend on checkpoint selection: with the final 400k checkpoints the gap is
\GapFinal{} [\GapFinalLo, \GapFinalHi] (\GapFinalRootsPositive{} of \GapRoots{}
roots). % CHECK per-scenario claim against tables/main_per_scenario at final
Per scenario (Table~\ref{tab:perscen}), the bandit is ahead in every
scenario except the double-failure scenario, where the two are close; the
largest gap is on the 24-hour \texttt{full\_day} scenario. Both learners
dominate the static, random and CSPF baselines; % CHECK greedy root counts
 the bandit also beats greedy
on every root, whereas PPO beats greedy on only some.

The most informative comparison is with a controller that does not learn at
all. MILP-track, which solves a min-max-utilization path-selection problem on
current telemetry every interval and moves one demand towards the solution,
reaches \TestMilp{} [\TestMilpLo, \TestMilpHi]. The bandit and MILP-track are
statistically indistinguishable (bandit $-$ MILP-track $=\BanditMinusMilp{}$
[\BanditMinusMilpLo, \BanditMinusMilpHi]); PPO falls short of MILP-track
($\PPOMinusMilp$ [\PPOMinusMilpLo, \PPOMinusMilpHi]). Operationally, the
bandit delivers \TestBanditDelivered\,\% of offered traffic with
\TestBanditReroutes{} reroutes per hour, against \TestPPODelivered\,\% and
\TestPPOReroutes{} for PPO and \TestMilpDelivered\,\% and \TestMilpReroutes{}
for MILP-track: the learned myopic policy achieves the optimizer's quality with
less than half its churn. % CHECK churn ratio
