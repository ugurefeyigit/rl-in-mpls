\begin{abstract}
Reinforcement learning (RL) is increasingly applied to traffic engineering
(TE), but whether TE decisions are sequential enough to need it is rarely
measured. We study an incremental MPLS-TE problem in which routing state
persists, one label-switched path may move per five-minute interval, moves
are costly, a hold-down follows each move and an action mask enforces
failures and protected-class capacity---formally a Markov decision process.
On a flow-level 18-router backbone, a masked contextual bandit that regresses
only the immediate reward of each action beats MaskablePPO on all
\TestBanditRoots{} training roots (paired return difference \Gap{}; $t$-interval
over roots [\GapTLo, \GapTHi]) and reaches the return of a non-learning
per-interval min-max-utilization MILP controller (difference
\BanditMinusMilp{}, [\BanditMinusMilpTLo, \BanditMinusMilpTHi]). Varying the
discount within each learner family points to the planning horizon rather
than the algorithm: PPO with $\gamma=0$ matches the bandit, while with
$\gamma=0.995$ it oscillates at hold-down expiry on \PPOFlappingRoots{} of \TestPPORoots{} roots and
reconfiguration costs make up most of its deficit; a bootstrapped value learner
with $\gamma=0.9$ is worse than the bandit. A tuning study does not close the
gap: the PPO configuration selected on one root's validation seeds falls
below the bandit on all \RQThreeTunedVsBanditRoots{} fresh roots. Clairvoyant lookahead explains why
there is little to plan for: a controller that re-plans every interval with
perfect knowledge of future traffic gains only \OracleThreeMinusOne{} return
from looking three intervals ahead instead of one, while knowing the next
interval exactly is worth \BanditBehindOracleOne{} over the bandit. When a
move's effect is delayed by one interval, the bandit's decision-time target
loses the move's benefit and the bandit collapses (\DelayBanditLZero{}
$\rightarrow$ \DelayBandit), below PPO on \DelayPPORootsBetter{} of \DelayPPORoots{} roots tested; the
optimization baseline stays ahead of both. A prior internal study's bandit
result reproduces on a different machine and software stack, while its PPO
result does not. In this setting, persistent state alone does not make RL's
planning horizon useful; the value of lookahead should be measured before
choosing a sequential learner.
\end{abstract}
