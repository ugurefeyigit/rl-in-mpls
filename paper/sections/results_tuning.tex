\subsection{RQ3: Is the gap an artifact of tuning, budget, shaping or the observation?}
\label{sec:rq3}
% Every number is generated; a guarded block prints "pending" until its
% experiment has finished, so the paper compiles at every stage.
\newcommand{\ifmacro}[3]{\ifcsname #1\endcsname #2\else #3\fi}

\paragraph{Learner noise within a root (E8).} Before reading the control
experiments, note how much a learner varies on identical training traffic.
\ifmacro{NoiseBanditSpread}{Retraining the default bandit on root 42 with two
other learner seeds (network initialization, exploration and replay sampling;
same episode-seed ledger) gives test returns of \NoiseBanditVals{} (original
first), a spread of \NoiseBanditSpread{} points. Differences of a few points
measured on one or two roots---E6 and E7 below---are therefore within learner
noise, and we treat them as inconclusive. The primary comparison is not
affected in this way: its $t$-interval is over training roots, each of which
varies the learner seed as well as the traffic.}{(pending)}

\paragraph{PPO tuning (E3).} Eight one-factor variations of PPO's
configuration were trained on root 42 and compared with the default on
validation seeds only (Appendix~\ref{app:hyper}).
\ifmacro{TuneEThreeRuns}{Of the \TuneEThreeRuns{} configurations, the
best by validation return is \texttt{\TuneBest} (\TuneBestVal, against
\TuneDefaultVal{} for the default and \TuneBanditValBest{} for the bandit on
the same root); Table~\ref{tab:tuning} lists all of them with their test
returns, which played no role in selection.}{(pending)}
On root 42 the selected configuration (entropy coefficient $0.03$) is ahead of
the bandit on validation and test, so H4 fails on the selection root, by a
margin smaller than the bandit's learner noise on that root. Root 42
is also the root on which default PPO was worst, and one-root selection is
noisy, so the configuration was retrained on the four other primary roots
(E3b).\ifmacro{RQThreeTunedVsBandit}{ There, tuned PPO minus the bandit is
\RQThreeTunedVsBandit{} (positive on \RQThreeTunedVsBanditRootsPositive{} of
\RQThreeTunedVsBanditRoots{} roots), and tuned minus default PPO is
\RQThreeTunedVsDefault{} (positive on \RQThreeTunedVsDefaultRootsPositive{}
of \RQThreeTunedVsDefaultRoots{}; Table~\ref{tab:rq3}). The improvement on
the selection root did not transfer; even there it depended on checkpoint
selection, since the final checkpoint on root 42 oscillates again. With the tuned
configuration PPO still oscillates on two of the four fresh roots.
The two variations that target the proposed mechanism behave differently on
the selection root: GAE $\lambda=0.8$, which shortens the advantage's credit
horizon while keeping $\gamma=0.995$, gives the second-best validation return
and a selected checkpoint that never reverses, but its final checkpoint is far
worse; reward normalization helps less. Five of the eight selected checkpoints
and six of the eight final ones reverse most of their moves. On one root this
is consistent with, but no
test of, the advantage-noise hypothesis.}{ (E3b pending.)}

\paragraph{Budget (E5).} \ifmacro{RQThreeBudgetPPOvsBandit}{With three times
the budget (1.2M transitions, root 42), PPO reaches \RQThreeBudgetPPOvsBanditA{}
against \RQThreeBudgetPPOvsBanditB{} for the bandit at 400k (difference
\RQThreeBudgetPPOvsBandit).\ifmacro{RQThreeBudgetBandit}{ The bandit itself
changes by \RQThreeBudgetBandit{} with the larger budget, which is within its
learner noise on this root.}{} PPO improves strongly with budget on this root,
its weakest at 400k, and its validation return was still rising at 1.2M; it
remains below the bandit at 400k under every learner seed we trained, but
whether it would catch up with more data is open.}{(pending)}

\paragraph{Shaping (E6).} The bandit is trained on the shaped reward, and
potential-based shaping is policy-invariant only for the discount it was
designed for; for a myopic learner it adds one step of lookahead on the
potential. \ifmacro{RQThreeNoShaping}{Training the bandit with the shaping
term removed and evaluating it on the primary reward changes its return by
\RQThreeNoShaping{} (\RQThreeNoShapingRootsPositive{} of
\RQThreeNoShapingRoots{} roots higher). Given the learner noise above, this does not show that shaping
hurts the bandit; it does show that the bandit's advantage over PPO does not
rest on shaping.}{(pending)}

\paragraph{Time of day (E7).} The open-loop diagnostic attributes the
non-myopic value to anticipation of exogenous change, and the observation has
no clock. If that is the missing ingredient for a sequential learner, adding
the time of day should help a $\gamma=0.9$ Q-learner more than the bandit
(H9, exploratory). \ifmacro{RQThreeTimeQ}{Adding the clock changes the
bandit's return by \RQThreeTimeBandit{} (\RQThreeTimeBanditRootsPositive{} of
\RQThreeTimeBanditRoots{} roots higher) and the Q-learner's by
\RQThreeTimeQ{} (\RQThreeTimeQRootsPositive{} of \RQThreeTimeQRoots{}
roots higher). The clock helps both learners a little and the myopic one at
least as much; both changes are within learner noise, so H9 is not
supported, and the clock does not create a benefit for planning that we can
detect.}{(pending)}
